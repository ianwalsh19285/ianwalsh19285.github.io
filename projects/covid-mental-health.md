<div class="project-copy">
  <p>
    This project analyzed how self-reported anxiety and depression symptoms differed across U.S. states and Census regions following the outbreak of COVID-19. Using CDC survey data from 2020 to 2023, it combined time-series visualization, regional comparison, and regression analysis to study how mental health patterns changed over time and how those patterns varied geographically.
  </p>

  <h2>What I built</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>A cleaned analysis workflow in Python using pandas, seaborn, plotly, and scikit-learn-oriented statistical tools.</li>
    <li>State- and region-level comparisons of anxiety and depression symptom rates using CDC Household Pulse Survey data.</li>
    <li>Time-series visualizations showing how national and regional symptom levels changed throughout the pandemic and its aftermath.</li>
    <li>A linear regression model using time and regional dummy variables, along with residual, Q-Q, and multicollinearity diagnostics to evaluate model assumptions.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Key outcomes</h2>
  <ul>
    <li>Found that anxiety and depression symptom rates differed meaningfully across U.S. regions after the start of COVID-19.</li>
    <li>Discovered that the Midwest and Northeast generally had lower combined symptom rates, while the South and West were consistently higher.</li>
    <li>Observed a major rise in symptoms during 2020 followed by a broad decline in 2021, with smaller fluctuations afterward.</li>
    <li>Showed that anxiety symptoms were consistently more prevalent than depression symptoms across the study period.</li>
    <li>Demonstrated that the regression captured broad regional differences, but did not fully satisfy key linear-model assumptions and explained only a modest share of overall variation.</li>
  </ul>
